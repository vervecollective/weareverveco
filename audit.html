Audit

The same sweep on every account, so the story is comparable.

When to run this. The ad account audit happens live, on the discovery call — you look at it together, no prep. The social sweep happens after the call and before the proposal. Pulling 90 days of performance is real work, and it is not work you do for someone who has not had a conversation with you yet.

What you see

Audit checklist — work top to bottom, count as you go

Every number below goes into your answers. Counting is what separates an audit from an opinion.

A · Profile & bio
Count CTAs in the bioShould be exactly 1. Zero means no path to buy. Two or more means none of them get taken.
Is the link live and relevant?Broken, generic homepage, or a link tree with 9 options are all failures. Note which.
Does the bio say who it is FOR?“Columbia plumber” beats “quality you can trust.” Count the words spent on them vs on the customer.
Category / contact buttons set?Call, directions, and hours present on the business profile — yes or no.
B · The hook — read the first line of the last 10 posts
How many open with a question, tension, or number?Score n/10. Under 3 means captions open with announcements, not hooks.
How many open with the brand talking about itself?“We are excited to announce” — count them. Every one is a scroll.
Average words before the value appearsOver ~12 and the hook is buried under the fold.
Repeated hook formulaIf every post opens the same way, note the pattern — it stops working.
C · The story test (StoryBrand — is the customer the hero?)
Who is the hero of the last 10 posts?Count customer-as-hero vs brand-as-hero. Brand-as-hero over half is the finding.
Is a problem named?If no post names a problem the customer has, there is nothing for them to recognise themselves in.
Is the brand positioned as the guide?Guide = empathy plus authority. Count posts showing either. Zero is common and worth saying.
Is there a plan?Can a stranger tell what happens if they call? Three steps or fewer, or it is not a plan.
Is failure ever named?“What it costs to keep doing nothing.” Almost nobody does this — it is usually the easiest win.
D · The caption — count per post
CTAs per caption1 is right. 0 = no ask. 3+ = noise. Report the average.
Ratio of value posts to ask postsHealthy is roughly 4:1. All-ask reads desperate; all-value never converts.
Hashtag count and relevanceCount them. 30 generic tags is a 2019 tactic and signals nobody is steering.
Reading levelIf a caption needs re-reading, it was written for the owner, not the customer.
E · The image — what is actually in frame
Faces vs product vs graphicCount each across 10 posts. Faces almost always outperform; note the split.
Is the subject identifiable in 1 second?Scroll fast. If you cannot tell what it is, neither can they.
Text-on-image legible at thumbnail?Count posts where it is not. Common and easy to fix.
Consistent look?Same crop, palette, or treatment — or does every post look like a different company?
Real photography vs stockCount stock images. Stock in a local service business reads as absent.
Vertical vs square vs landscapeCount non-vertical posts. On Reels and TikTok that is wasted canvas.
F · Cadence & format
Posts per week, this period vs lastTwo numbers. The change is the story.
Format mixStatic / carousel / video / story. All one format means nothing has been tested.
Longest gap between postsA three-week gap explains a reach collapse on its own.
Best and worst performing postName both and what separates them.
How to use it. Work A through F and write the numbers down as you go. Then answer the five questions below — every claim you make should point back to a number you counted. “Seven of ten posts make the brand the hero” is an audit. “Their content is too self-focused” is an opinion.
Cheat sheet — if you see this, write this
1 · The story
Brand is the hero in most posts“[n] of the last 10 posts are about the business rather than the customer. Nothing on the feed names a problem a customer would recognise as theirs.”
Zero or multiple CTAs“[n] CTAs per caption on average. [Zero means attention arrives with nowhere to go / Three or more means none of them get taken.]”
Hooks are announcements“[n] of 10 captions open with the brand announcing something. The first line is the only line most people read, and it is being spent on the wrong subject.”
Stock imagery in a local business“[n] of 10 images are stock. For a local service business that reads as absence — the people are the product here.”
No plan, no next step“Nothing on the profile tells a stranger what happens if they call. Without three simple steps, interest has nowhere to land.”
Failure never named“No post names what it costs to keep doing nothing. That is usually the cheapest thing to add and the fastest to move.”
Posting stopped or went irregular“Posting was consistent through [month], then dropped to [n] a week. Reach followed it down.”
Posting steady, reach falling“Output has held steady but reach has fallen [n]% over 90 days — the audience is there, the content is not landing.”
Reach fine, engagement near zero“Content is being seen and ignored. That is a message problem, not a distribution problem.”
Followers growing, engagement flat“The follower count is going up while engagement stays flat, which usually means the new followers are not buyers.”
Everything is one format“Almost all output is [static images / reshared posts]. There is no format variety to test against.”
Nothing has changed at all“Ninety days of consistent, unchanging output. Nothing is broken and nothing is improving — this account is idling.”
Active on one platform only“All effort is on [platform]. That is fine if the customer is there, and expensive if they are not.”
2 · What is genuinely working
One format outperforms“[Format] posts consistently outperform everything else, at roughly [n]x the engagement.”
Faces beat products“Posts with a person in them outperform product shots. The audience responds to people here.”
A topic keeps landing“Anything about [topic] outperforms. That is a signal worth building on.”
Consistency itself“The posting discipline is real. Most accounts this size cannot sustain it, and it is a foundation to build on.”
Good bio / profile setup“The profile is set up correctly — clear bio, working link, right category. That part is done.”
Genuinely nothing“Nothing has cleared [n]% engagement in 90 days. I would rather say that plainly than invent a strength.”
3 · The gap costing the most
No link, no offer, no CTA“There is no path from a post to a purchase. Attention arrives and has nowhere to go.”
Engagement rate under 0.5%“Content is reaching the wrong people. Below half a percent is a targeting problem, not a volume problem.”
All awareness, no conversion“Everything is top-of-funnel. Nothing asks anyone to do anything.”
Wrong platform for the buyer“The effort is on [platform] and the customer is on [other]. The work is fine, the room is wrong.”
No tracking installed“There is no conversion tracking, so nobody can tell which of this is working. That has to be fixed before anything is scaled.”
Competitors running ads, they aren't“[Competitor] is running paid; this account is not. They are buying the attention this account is trying to earn.”
Inconsistent brand“Tone and look change post to post, so nothing compounds into recognition.”
4 · What we would do (three moves, tied to the gap)
Gap = no conversion path“Add one clear offer, put it in the bio and in every third post, and install tracking so we can see what converts.”
Gap = low engagement rate“Cut posting volume, raise the quality bar, and test [winning format] for four weeks before changing anything else.”
Gap = no tracking“Install conversion tracking first. Establish a baseline for 30 days. Change nothing until we can measure it.”
Gap = wrong platform“Hold [current] at maintenance level and run a 60-day test on [other] before shifting real budget.”
Gap = competitors buying attention“Start a small paid test against the audience already engaging organically, at a spend level we can afford to learn from.”
Gap = inconsistency“Set one format, one voice, and a fixed cadence for 90 days. Consistency compounds; variety this early does not.”
5 · The one thing to fix first (two weeks, visible)
Nothing is measured“Install conversion tracking this month. Everything else is guesswork until that exists.”
No offer anywhere“Put one clear offer in the bio and pin a post about it this week.”
One format clearly wins“For the next two weeks, post only [format] and nothing else. Then we look again.”
Posting has stalled“Get back to [n] posts a week for two weeks before we change anything about what is posted.”
Spending with no oversight“Pause the campaigns that have not converted in 30 days. Stop the bleed before optimising anything.”
Rules. Never invent a number — if you did not see it, do not write it. Describe changes, not states: “reach fell 60%” beats “reach is 12,000”. Pick exactly one gap and one first action; two of either means the audit is not finished. And never promise a result — observations are defensible, forecasts are not.
