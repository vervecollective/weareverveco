01 · Before the work

A lead becomes a qualified conversation

Outreach splits in two. A business already running ads gets a free account review, scored against a six-point scorecard. A business spending nothing gets a growth readiness call. Which track applies is decided in the outreach email itself.

An agency that pitches everyone the same way spends the first meeting discovering what it could have known beforehand.

02 · Closing

One contract, signed once

The Client Services Agreement carries its scope as Exhibit A and is signed in a single event. Scope, timeline, fee, payment structure and revision rounds fill in automatically from the engagement, so what the client signs and what the platform tracks cannot drift apart.

The agreement states that ad spend is paid by the client directly to Google or Meta. It never touches Verve's books and never appears on a Verve invoice — only as a reference figure. That separation is where small agencies most often lose trust.

Note the revisions line. Revision rounds attach to creative elements, not to engagements — two editing rounds per video, per photo set, per social asset, per aerial deliverable. Golden Crane is paid media only, so the agreement says there are none rather than promising something with nothing to apply it to.

03 · Getting paid

The deposit unlocks the work, not the invoice

Signing raises a payment schedule from the structure in the agreement — 50/50, 40/30/30, or a monthly retainer. Each part is its own invoice. The balance cannot be sent before the deposit clears, and production does not begin until money has landed.

Enforced in code rather than remembered. Management fee and ad spend are always separate lines.

04 · Doing the work

Files organise themselves

Creating an engagement creates its folders. Each one records who is meant to fill it, so an empty folder reads as a prompt rather than a void. Craft folders appear when a contractor is actually assigned that craft.

Private by default, served through short-lived signed links. A client cannot reach another client's files by guessing a path — authorisation lives in the database, not in the interface.

05 · Talking about it

Conversations attached to the work

Messaging is native, not a chat widget bolted on. A conversation can be tied to an engagement, and a file dropped into it lands in that engagement's folders automatically. Threads, reactions, read state, presence and search all run on the same permission model as everything else.

Messages carry a participant type, so an AI participant can join later as a real member with real permissions rather than as a separate bot. The seam is built; the integration is deliberately future work.

06 · Bringing in help

Contractors clear a gate before they see anything

A contractor is not bookable until the paperwork is complete. The system computes that from what is actually on file; nobody decides it from memory.

Contractors are paid within 15 days of approval whether or not the client has paid. A contractor's rate is between them and Verve — a higher rate costs Verve margin, never the client. Credit on published work is on by default.

07 · Proving it worked

Reporting the client can read

Google Ads and Meta performance is pulled on a schedule and stored in Verve's own database, so a report never depends on an API answering at the moment a client opens the page — and last month's numbers survive a client revoking access.

Golden Crane shows why both platforms run at once. Meta buys reach at a fraction of the cost per click; Google buys intent that converts. Cost per conversion is the number worth arguing about.

08 · Underneath

Deliberately small

No framework, no bundler, no build step. Static pages, one shared stylesheet and script, deployed straight from a repository — live about thirty seconds after a push. Every part of it can be understood and replaced by one person.

DatabasePostgres on Supabase. Row-level security on every table, so authorisation lives with the data rather than in the interface.
BackendDeno edge functions for contracts, billing, notifications and the ad-platform sync. Every secret stays server-side.
FrontendStatic HTML and vanilla JavaScript on Netlify.
ContractsSignWell, generated from templates and prefilled from the engagement.
PaymentsStripe for invoicing, QuickBooks for the books. Ad spend passes through neither.
AdvertisingGoogle Ads API and Meta Marketing API, cached daily into Postgres.
PipelineHubSpot, connected to the platform's own engagement records.
